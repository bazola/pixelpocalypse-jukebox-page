Songs on demand · runs on your own machine

PixelpocalypseJukebox

Type what you want and get a song played by real synth plugins. Then edit every note in the browser, ask your AI for changes, or leave the jukebox running and let it keep writing new ones.

An LLM on your own machine plans the song: tempo, key, chords, sections and which sound plays what. Code writes the notes, renders them through VST3 instruments, and critics listen to the result.

See what it does Read the manual Get it running

> a deep funky groove in C minor with a fat bass and chord stabs

From a sentence to a song

  1. PlanYour AI picks tempo, key, chords, sections and sounds.
  2. WriteCode writes the notes. The seed decides them.
  3. RenderEvery part plays through real VST3 plugins.
  4. CritiqueCritics measure the audio and ask for up to 3 fixes.
  5. MasterThe song is balanced and brought to -14 LUFS.
  6. EditIt opens in the editor, and everything can change.

A three-minute song takes a few minutes, and you watch every step as it happens. A quick draft skips the critics and is playable in one or two.

Make your selection

A

Write songs

  • Generate from a promptSay what you want, in your own words.
  • 16 writersFunk bassist, techno minimalist, chiptune composer, film composer and twelve more, each with their own habits.
  • Remix and build aroundMake a new song from an old one, or keep a passage and have the rest written around it.
  • Seeds and ensemblesGet the same notes again, or hand a band that sounds right the next song.
B

The jukebox

  • A mood that never runs outGive it a mood or a short line, and it keeps writing and playing new songs.
  • Always one song aheadThe next song is being made while this one plays.
  • Channels that learnName a channel and it learns from the songs you star there.
  • Keep what you loveCopy any song from its history into your songs, ready to edit.
C

Edit every note

  • Timeline and chord laneCopy, paste and duplicate sections. Change a bar's chord and the parts follow it.
  • Piano roll and drum gridDraw notes and hits, and shape their velocity with drawn ramps.
  • Regenerate and 4 variationsRewrite a few bars, or hear four versions in the mix and pick one.
  • KeysPlay any sound from the computer keyboard or a USB MIDI keyboard, and record snippets.
D

Revise with your AI

  • Ask for a change"Make the chorus bigger", and get a map of each change to accept or reject.
  • The critics' roundsLoudness, levels, kick against bass, key fit: every measurement, round by round.
  • Partial rendersOnly the parts you changed are rendered again.
  • Undo and historyEvery save is a version, and the song remembers what made each one.
E

Shape the sound

  • LibraryThousands of sounds from 29 instruments, by role, tag and plugin, each one a click to hear.
  • Tags that steer the AIA prompt saying "fat gritty bass" finds the sounds tagged fat and gritty.
  • MixerLevels, pan, effects, and swapping a track's sound or drum kit.
  • Master-14 LUFS through a true-peak limiter, with a loudness-matched Bypass.
F

Take it anywhere

  • ExportWAV, MP3 and a MIDI file, with the song's credits and history.
  • StemsA 48 kHz, 24-bit WAV per track, all lined up at 0, plus the MIDI.
  • Command lineGenerate, revise, render and export from a shell, overnight batches included.
  • HTTP and MCPDrive it from scripts, or let an AI assistant make and edit songs.

What it needs

  • Windows. The plugin builds and paths are Windows ones.
  • A local LLM through Ollama or LM Studio, with a 16k context. No cloud account, no API key, no bill per song.
  • About 3.7 GB of free plugins and sample libraries, fetched and checksummed by one script.
  • Optional: Surrealistic MG-1 Plus, a paid plugin, for its basses.
